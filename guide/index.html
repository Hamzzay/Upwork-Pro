Upwork Pro: the team guide

How to do the daily work, with Claude and without it, how to add and manage everything proposals are built from, and how to read the numbers. Each picture has numbers on it; the list under the picture says what each one is. Click a picture to see it full size.

Start here

Upwork Pro takes an Upwork job from the moment you find it to the day the client answers: it screens the job against our rules, picks the past projects that prove we can do it, writes the proposal in the right profile's voice, and then keeps track of what the client did. Everything it learns ends up in Reports, so we can see what works.

You areYou can
EmployeeScreen jobs, write and send proposals, update their status, see your own jobs and reports, read the library and the writing guide, connect your own Claude.
ManagerAll of that for everyone's jobs, plus add and edit projects, industries, proposal types, the writing guide and signals.
AdminEverything: also the tag dictionary, Upwork profiles, Loom videos, rules, gate instructions, settings, users and logs.

How every page is laid out

Every page works the same way, so once you know one you know them all.

  1. 1The sidebar. Work is what you do every day. Library is what proposals are built from. Proposal setup is how they are written. Admin is for admins.
  2. 2The title of the page, with one line under it saying what the page is for.
  3. 3The page's main button is always at the top right.
  4. 4You, your role, and Sign out.

On a phone

Everything works on a phone. The sidebar becomes a Menu button.

  1. 1Press Menu to open the links.
Three things are the same on every list: a search box and filters on top (they apply as you type, and Clear filters appears once something is filtered), a row opens when you click it, and a row's buttons are at its right end. Deleting is always inside the Edit window, never in the row.

The daily job, without Claude

One job, five steps: decide, projects, profile, proposal (choose its type, then review it), and tracking. The job page shows the step you are on and the button for it is always at the top right of the tab row.

1. Screen a job

Open Screen a job in the sidebar.

  1. 1On Upwork, select the whole job page (Cmd + A, Cmd + C) and paste it here. Include the "About the client" part: the screening depends on it.
  2. 2Paste the job's link too. It is saved with the job and stops the same job being screened twice.
  3. 3Press Screen this job (or Cmd + Enter).

It screens in about a minute

You can leave the page; the result is saved and the job is in your Jobs list.

  1. 1The page updates by itself when the screening is done.

2. Decide: continue or skip

The gate answers Pass, Flag (needs a human look) or Fail (breaks a rule). It advises; you decide.

  1. 1The tabs of the job. A tick means the step is done. Steps you cannot reach yet are grey.
  2. 2Skip or Continue. Every button of the job page is here, at the right end of the tab row.
  3. 3The decision. On a Pass, just continue.
  4. 4The result and why.

Under it you find how well the job fits us and notes for the proposal.

When the gate says Flag or Fail

You can still continue, but you must write why. The reason stays on the record, where managers can read it.

  1. 1The result.
  2. 2The reason someone gave for continuing.
  3. 3Each rule that fired, with the value behind it (for example the client's real hire rate).

3. Pick the projects

The job is tagged, and the projects that share the most with it are listed: same industry first.

  1. 1The two recommended projects are already ticked.
  2. 2Click a card to tick or untick it. To swap, untick one first.
  3. 3Press Confirm and continue.
  4. 4How many you have picked, out of how many you may.

4. Pick the profile

Which Upwork profile sends this proposal. The proposal is written in that profile's voice, with its rate, links and rules.

  1. 1Click the profile.
  2. 2Press Confirm and continue.

Choose the type of proposal

Nothing is written until you choose. The types that suit this job come first, each with the reason it fits.

  1. 1The suggested type: the best fit for what the job post says. It is already selected.
  2. 2The other types offered, with why each fits or does not. Click one to choose it instead. "Ruled out" means something in the post speaks against it; you can still pick it.
  3. 3Show all types lists every proposal type.
  4. 4Press Write the proposal. Only the type you chose is written.

The proposal is written for you

This takes a few minutes. You can leave and come back.

  1. 1It writes the type you chose, with your projects and profile.

5. Review the proposal

Read it. It is your proposal: change anything.

  1. 1The proposal. Edit it like any document.
  2. 2Every save, chat change and restore is kept as a version. Pick an older one here to see it or bring it back.
  3. 3Chat with the AI to change it: "make it shorter", "add a question about their calendar". Each answer is saved as a new version.
  4. 4Copy puts the text on your clipboard, ready to paste into Upwork. Finish proposal when you are happy with it.
  5. 5Open this to see the signals it read and how every type ranked. You can write it again with another type.

Above the text, a yellow line lists things to check before sending (for example a figure the writer could not find in our facts).

Send it on Upwork, then mark it as sent

Paste the proposal into Upwork and submit it there. Then come back to the Tracking tab.

  1. 1Mark as sent (later this button says Update status). Edit details is for Connects, boost, the Loom video and notes.
  2. 2Where the proposal stands now.
  3. 3Everything that happened, with its date.
  4. 4Connects, boost, the Loom video and your notes.

Updating the status takes one click

Do this every time something happens on Upwork. It is what the reports are built from.

  1. 1Pick what happened: Sent, Viewed, Chat opened, Interview, or how it ended. The next likely one is already chosen.
  2. 2It is set to now. Change it only if it happened earlier.
  3. 3Save.

When a proposal is lost, say why

Not hired, No response, Withdrawn and Job closed all need a reason, so we can learn from them.

  1. 1The outcome.
  2. 2The reason, from the list.
  3. 3Anything the client said, in your own words.

Record what it cost

On the Tracking tab, press Edit details.

  1. 1The Connects the proposal cost.
  2. 2Extra Connects if you boosted it.
  3. 3The Loom video you sent with it, if any. This is how we learn whether videos help.
  4. 4Notes.

The rest of the job page

  1. 1The title, the gate result and the step.
  2. 2Overview, the job post as fields, the five steps, and History.
  3. 3What is next.
  4. 4The button for it.

Job post: the posting as fields

The whole post, read into fields at the same time as the screening: terms, skills, description, screening questions, the client and their history.

  1. 1The full pasted text is one click away at the bottom.

History: every step and who did it

When something looks wrong, look here first.

  1. 1Each step with how long after the paste it happened, who did it, and each AI call.

Finding and following your jobs

Every job, from screening to outcome.

  2. 2Click a tile to see only Pass, Flag or Fail.

  4. 4Click a row to open the job. Click "Where it stands" or the result for a quick look without leaving the list.

  2. 2Clear filters removes them all. It only shows while something is filtered.

Following up: the Submitted tab

Open this tab every day.

  1. 1Submitted.
  2. 2How far each proposal got: Sent, Viewed, Chat, Interview.
  3. 3Gone quiet: sent and nothing recorded for 5 days. Click it to see them.
  4. 4Update the status in one click.

A quick look at a job

Click "Where it stands" on any row.

  1. 1The job's journey so far, without opening it.

Choose your columns

Press Columns. Your choice is remembered for each tab, on this computer.

  1. 1Tick what you want to see.

Export

Press Export to get exactly the jobs on screen (with your filters) as Excel or CSV, with every detail: the post, the result, the proposal text, every status and date.

  1. 1Excel or CSV. Up to 5,000 jobs.

Working with Claude

You can do the same work inside Claude with the Stackup proposals plugin: Claude screens the job, matches projects and writes the proposal, and saves everything into Upwork Pro under your name. The library, rules and writing guide it uses are the ones in Upwork Pro, so both ways give the same result.

Connect your Claude (once)

Open Connect Claude.

  1. 1The connector link and the steps. In Claude: Settings, Connectors, Add custom connector, name it Upwork Pro, paste the link, press Connect, then sign in with your own Upwork Pro email and password and press Allow.
  2. 2Copy the link.

Everything your Claude saves is recorded as you. Never share your sign-in or a token.

Or use a token (when Upwork Pro runs on your own computer)

  1. 1Name the token and press Make a token. Copy it at once: it is shown one time only.
  2. 2Paste the settings it gives you into Claude Desktop.
  3. 3Your tokens. Revoke one the moment you think someone else has it.

Install the plugin

  1. Get the file stackup-proposals-0.4.0.plugin from your admin.
  2. In Claude, open Plugins and add the file.
  3. Restart Claude.

Adding and managing the library

The library is what proposals are built from. Keep it true: the writer only says what the library says.

Projects

Our delivered work. Managers and admins can add and edit.

  1. 1Search, and filter by industry, tag, content (for example "No case study") or status.
  2. 2Click a row to see the whole project.
  3. 3Edit.
  4. 4Add project.

Add or edit a project

  1. 1The name, as we call it.
  2. 2The link a proposal shows: usually the landing page, or the store or live system link.
  3. 3What we built. The writer describes the project from this, so write it plainly and truthfully.
  4. 4Add project. The head and the buttons stay in view while you scroll.

The case study summary is where results and figures go. Only figures written there may appear in a proposal.

Industries and tags decide when a project is suggested

Scroll down in the same window.

  1. 1The industries the project belongs to. Projects from the job's own industry are suggested first.
  2. 2The tags. A job is matched to the projects that share its tags, so tag honestly: only what the project really did.
  3. 3Save.

To stop using a project without losing it, untick Active. Delete is in this window too, for mistakes only.

A project's page

  1. 1Back to the list, with your filters as you left them.
  2. 2The name and whether it is active.
  3. 3Edit project.

Industries

  1. 1Search and status.
  2. 2Edit.
  3. 3Add industry.

Edit an industry

  1. 1The name.
  2. 2Related industries, separated by commas. For a job in this industry, projects from these come right after projects from the same one.
  3. 3The projects in it.

Tag dictionary (admin)

The words jobs and projects are described with.

  1. 1Tags and their categories.
  2. 2Search, category and status.
  3. 3The score: what a shared tag is worth when matching. Type a new number and it is saved. 0 means ignored.
  4. 4Edit, and Disable to stop using a tag without deleting it.
  5. 5Add tag.

Edit a tag

  1. 1The name.
  2. 2The score, 0 to 10.
  3. 3What it means. The AI reads this when it tags a job, so one clear sentence matters.

Upwork profiles (admin)

The profiles we apply from.

  1. 1Search, status, and "Needs filling in" to find profiles without a rate or headline.
  2. 2Edit and Disable.
  3. 3Add profile.

Edit a profile

Every field here is used by the writer. An empty field means the writer has nothing to say about it.

  1. 1The name, exactly as on Upwork.
  2. 2The default hourly rate, and the lowest rate.
  3. 3What this profile sells. Used to suggest a profile for a job.
  4. 4Delete is here, for mistakes only. Disable a profile instead if it has ever been used.

Further down: voice, signature, the Upwork stats allowed in proposals, profile rules, and certifications (used only when a client requires one).

Loom videos (admin)

Short videos per profile. On a job's Profile step, each profile shows the video that fits the job best.

  1. 1Search, profile, status and "Not tagged".
  2. 2Add video for that profile.
  3. 3Add video.

Add a Loom video

  1. 1A title that says what the video shows.
  2. 2The Loom link.
  3. 3The job tags it suits. A video with no tags is never suggested.

When you send a video with a proposal, record it on the job (Tracking, Edit details). That is what lets Reports compare proposals with a video against those without.

  2. 2Click a signal to see its values.

  1. 1Edit signal (admin).
  2. 2Each value: how to spot it in a post, and what the proposal should do when it applies.

Writing guide

How every proposal is written, by Upwork Pro and by Claude alike. One copy, kept here.

  1. 1Search, and show only proposal types, the shared rules, or what needs setting up (types without samples).
  2. 2A proposal type. Click it to edit (managers and admins).
  3. 3Add proposal type.

A proposal type

  1. 1The name.
  2. 2Priority. Tens set the group: 10 is tried first, then 20, 30, 40.
  3. 3The format: the structure and rules the proposal must follow. Keep the "Chosen when" and "Length" lines.
  4. 4Save type.

When a type is chosen, and its samples

Further down the same page.

  1. 1The signals this type suits. Required: it is only chosen when these match. Rules it out: a match excludes it. Supporting: each match adds a point.
  2. 2Sample proposals that worked. The writer uses up to three as a reference for tone and structure, never for facts.

The shared rules

Writing rules, banned phrases, modules, screening answers and the checklist apply to every type.

  1. 1The text. Edit it here.
  2. 2Every save is kept. Search the versions, and Load one to bring it back.
  3. 3Turn a document off without deleting it.
  4. 4Save. It applies from the next proposal, everywhere.

Rules (admin)

The fail and flag rules of the gate.

  1. 1Search, fail or flag, status, and "Never fired".
  2. 2Edit rewords a rule. Retire stops it without deleting it.
  3. 3Add a rule. It gets the next free code.

A code is never reused. If the meaning changes, retire the rule and add a new one, so old jobs keep their meaning.

Edit a rule

  1. 1The rule.
  2. 2How to apply it: the exceptions and how to measure it. The AI reads this with the rule.

Dashboard

How the team is doing right now.

  2. 2The headline numbers. Click any of them to open those jobs.
  3. 3The funnel, from screened to hired.
  4. 4How fast we get from paste to proposal.

Reports

What is working and what is not.

  2. 2The rates, of sent proposals: viewed, chat opened, interview, hired, and what a hire costs in Connects.

Break it down

The same jobs, cut 27 ways: by profile, person, proposal type, project, industry, tag, signal, rule, Loom video, day sent, client country and more.

  1. 1Choose the report, and "At least 5 sent" to hide groups too small to trust.
  2. 2Click a column title to sort by it.
  3. 3Click a row to open the jobs behind it.
  4. 4Every report as a small card. Click one to open it in full.

A rate is only as good as what was recorded. If statuses are not updated, the reports are wrong. A group with three sent proposals proves nothing: look for groups with ten or more.

  1. 1Activity: who did what. AI calls: every call to the AI, how long it took and whether it failed.
  2. 2Filters.

Five habits that keep it working

  1. Paste the whole job page, with the client section. The gate can only judge what it sees.
  2. Update the status the same day: Sent, Viewed, Chat opened, Interview, and the outcome with its reason.
  3. Record Connects, boost and the Loom video on every sent proposal.
  4. Write a real reason when you continue past a flag or a fail. Someone will read it.
  5. Fix the library, not the proposal. If the writer says something wrong about a project or a profile, correct it in the library so the next proposal is right too.
