Upwork Pro: the team guide

How to do the daily work, with Claude and without it, how to add and manage everything proposals are built from, and how to read the numbers. Each picture has numbers on it; the list under the picture says what each one is. Click a picture to see it full size. Setting up a fresh copy? Read Setting up from zero.

Start here

Upwork Pro takes an Upwork job from the moment you find it to the day the client answers: it screens the job against our rules, picks the past projects that prove we can do it, writes the proposal in the right profile's voice, and then keeps track of what the client did. Everything it learns ends up in Reports, so we can see what works.

You areYou can
EmployeeScreen jobs, write and send proposals, update their status, see your own jobs and reports, read the library and the writing guide, connect your own Claude.
ManagerAll of that for everyone's jobs, plus add and edit projects, industries, proposal types, the writing guide and signals, and read the Flag review.
AdminEverything: also the tag dictionary, Upwork profiles, Loom videos, rules, gate instructions, the reviewer, settings, users and logs. Only an admin can discard a job (and restore it).

The daily job, without Claude

One job, four steps: decide, projects, profile and proposal (choose its type, then review it). After it is sent, you keep its status up to date on the Overview tab. The job page shows the step you are on and the button for it is always at the top right of the tab row.

1. Screen a job

Open Screen a job in the sidebar.

  1. 1On Upwork, select the whole job page (Cmd + A, Cmd + C) and paste it here. Include the "About the client" part: the screening depends on it.
  2. 2Paste the job's link too. It is saved with the job and stops the same job being screened twice.
  3. 3Press Screen this job (or Cmd + Enter).

It screens in about a minute

You can leave the page; the result is saved and the job is in your Jobs list.

  1. 1The page updates by itself when the screening is done.

2. Decide: continue or skip

The gate answers Pass, Flag (needs a human look) or Fail (breaks a rule). It advises; you decide.

  1. 1The tabs of the job. A tick means the step is done. Steps you cannot reach yet are grey.
  2. 2Skip or Continue. Every button of the job page is here, at the right end of the tab row.
  3. 3The decision. On a Pass, just continue.
  4. 4The result and why.

Under it you find how well the job fits us and notes for the proposal.

When the gate says Flag or Fail

You can still continue, but you must say why. The reason stays on the record, where managers can read it, and the reasons are counted in the Flag review.

  1. 1The question: continue despite the flag or fail?
  2. 2Quick reasons. One click writes a reason into the box; click it again to take it out. Pick as many as fit. An admin sets the list in Settings.
  3. 3The reason. Add your own words around the quick reasons ("They asked for us by name.").
  4. 4Each rule that fired, with the value behind it (for example the client's real hire rate).

A PASS, or a FLAG with no more than two flags, is matched with projects by itself straight after the screening. More flags, or a FAIL, wait until someone continues, so nothing is spent on jobs we are likely to skip. The number is a setting.

Skipping a job: say why

Press Skip and a small window asks for the reason. It takes two seconds and it is what lets us report on the jobs we pass on.

  1. 1Pick the quick reason that fits.
  2. 2Add a note if there is more to say.
  3. 3Skip job. The job moves to Not pursued, with your reason on it.

3. Pick the projects

The job is read for the work it needs, and the projects that prove that work are listed. A project is only recommended when it shows the same kind of work; among those, the same industry comes first.

  1. 1Skip tech stack tags. Turn it on to match the projects again without the job's languages, frameworks and databases; everything else counts as usual. Use it when the stack does not matter to this client. It cannot change once a proposal is written.
  2. 2A recommended project, already ticked. Under its name: why it fits, whether it is the same industry, and the tags it shares with the job.
  3. 3The other matches. Click a card to tick or untick it. To swap, untick one first.
  4. 4What the job needs. Press Confirm and continue at the top right; the count there says how many you have picked out of how many you may.

The list is the same one the Screening tab shows under "Projects that fit", so the two never disagree.

Not there? Show more

Scroll to the end of the list.

  1. 1Show 10 more projects lists the next best matches.
  2. 2Open this to see the tags the job was given, and so why these projects came up.

4. Pick the profile

Which Upwork profile sends this proposal. The proposal is written in that profile's voice, with its rate, links and rules.

  1. 1Click the profile.
  2. 2Press Confirm and continue.

Choose the type of proposal

Nothing is written until you choose. The types that suit this job come first, each with the reason it fits.

  1. 1The suggested type: the best fit for what the job post says. It is already selected.
  2. 2The other types offered, with why each fits or does not. Click one to choose it instead. "Ruled out" means something in the post speaks against it; you can still pick it.
  3. 3Show all types lists every proposal type.
  4. 4Press Write the proposal. Only the type you chose is written.

Send a Loom video with it?

On the same screen, under the types. When the job post asks for a video, the one that fits best is already selected.

  1. 1Pick a video of the sending profile, or No Loom video. The link of the one you pick is written into the proposal, and it is recorded on the job for the reports.
  2. 2Then press Write the proposal.

If the client asks for a video and the profile has none, the screen says so: an admin adds videos under Loom videos.

The proposal is written for you

Usually it is there within seconds: a first draft is written in the background as soon as the projects are matched, and only the parts you chose differently are redone. You can leave and come back.

  1. 1It writes the type you chose, with your projects, profile and video.

5. Review the proposal

Read it. It is your proposal: change anything.

  1. 1The proposal: the cover letter only. Edit it like any document.
  2. 2Every save, chat change, update and restore is kept as a version. Pick an older one here to see it or bring it back.
  3. 3Loom video: pick another video, or none, at any time. Only the video line changes, at once; nothing is written again.
  4. 4Beside the proposal, one card with a tab for each tool. Chat with the AI to change it: "make it shorter", "add a question about their calendar". Each answer is saved as a new version. Feedback records what is wrong in it, to fix the setup later.
  5. 5Review: a second reader scores the proposal out of 10, and the score shows on the tab. See below. Experience appears when the client asks for something we have no record of: answer Yes or No there.
  6. 6Copy cover letter puts the letter on your clipboard. Change type writes it again as another proposal type. Save and mark sent when it is submitted on Upwork.

Above the text, a yellow line lists things to check before sending (for example a figure the writer could not find in our facts), and a blue button says how many questions about our experience are waiting. Under the text, How this was written opens the signals and the proposal type ranking.

The screening answers, apart from the proposal

When the client asks questions in Upwork's own questions section, each is answered apart from the cover letter, on its own Screening answers tab next to the Cover letter tab. A dot on a tab means unsaved changes. Editing or removing text in the proposal never touches them.

  1. 1The questions and how many.
  2. 2The answer, in its own box. Edit it here; Save version saves the letter and the answers together.
  3. 3Copy each answer into its own box on Upwork.

Changing the projects, the profile or the video after writing

A proposal is made of parts: the letter, one block per project, the video line and the sign off. Change the projects or the profile on their tabs, or the video here, and only that part is redone, as a new version: a new project takes a few seconds, a video or a profile no time at all. The letter stays as it was. To write the letter again in another profile's voice, use Change type or write it again.

  1. 1Once you have edited the text yourself, your text is never overwritten.
  2. 2Instead, the new parts are offered here.
  3. 3Put it in adds it at the end of your text: move it where it belongs, then save.
  4. 4Copy it to paste it yourself.
  5. 5Dismiss when you do not want them.

The independent review

As soon as a proposal is written, a separate reader checks it against the job, the profile, the projects and our writing rules. It did not write the proposal, so it does not go easy on it. It is advice and never forces a change: you can always copy the proposal as it is. How it reviews is set by an admin on the Reviewer page.

  1. 1The score out of 10, and which version it is for.
  2. 2The verdict in a few lines: send it or not, and why.
  3. 3A suggestion: what to change, in words the AI can act on. There are up to three.
  4. 4Apply this sends that suggestion to the chat. The AI revises the proposal and saves it as a new version.
  5. 5Review again scores the newest version. After a change, the panel tells you the review is of an older version.

Further down, What hurts it and What works open to show each point. A low score most often means the proposal claims something our project facts do not say: fix that first.

  2. 2The Connects it cost, and any boost.
  3. 3The Loom video you sent with it, if any. This is how we learn whether videos help.
  4. 4Save.

There is no Tracking tab any more. After this, the job's status lives on its Overview tab.

Working with Claude

You can do the same work inside Claude with the Stackup proposals plugin: Claude screens the job, matches projects and writes the proposal, and saves everything into Upwork Pro under your name. The library, rules and writing guide it uses are the ones in Upwork Pro, so both ways give the same result.

Connect your Claude (once)

Open Connect Claude.

  1. 1The connector link and the steps. In Claude: Settings, Connectors, Add custom connector, name it Upwork Pro, paste the link, press Connect, then sign in with your own Upwork Pro email and password and press Allow.
  2. 2Copy the link.

Everything your Claude saves is recorded as you. Never share your sign-in or a token.

Or use a token (when Upwork Pro runs on your own computer)

  1. 1Name the token and press Make a token. Copy it at once: it is shown one time only.
  2. 2Paste the settings it gives you into Claude Desktop.
  3. 3Your tokens. Revoke one the moment you think someone else has it.

Get the plugin

On the same page, under the connection. Everyone can download it.

  1. 1Download the plugin. You get one file, named with its version.
  2. 2Add it in Claude: open Plugins, add a plugin from a file, choose the file, then restart Claude.

When this card shows a newer version than the one you installed, download it again and add it the same way.

Changing the setup from Claude (admins and managers)

Admins can add and change the setup by talking to Claude instead of filling in the windows above: projects, industries, tags, Upwork profiles, Loom videos, rules, signals, proposal types and their samples, the writing guide, the gate instructions, the reviewer and settings. Managers can do from Claude what they can do in the app: projects, industries, proposal types, samples and the writing guide. Employees cannot change the setup.

You need the plugin, version 0.11.0 or newer for the reviewer (Connect Claude, Download the plugin), and your Claude connected with your own sign-in.

Nothing raw goes in

The setup is read by the AI on every job, so one careless project or tag hurts every proposal after it. Claude therefore always works in the same seven steps, and you should expect each of them:

  1. It looks at what exists, so nothing is added twice. If the thing is already there, it offers to update it.
  2. It shows you two or three good examples of the same kind from our own library, so you see the shape we keep them in.
  3. It asks for the information, one topic at a time. Give it in any form: notes, a pasted document, a link.
  4. It tells you what is complete and what is missing. It never fills a gap with a guess: no invented results, numbers, clients, rates or links. What you do not know stays empty.
  5. It verifies what it can: that the tags, industries and names exist, that the name is free, that the links are the ones you gave.
  6. It rewrites everything in our standard format and shows you the final version exactly as it will be saved, with a short list of what it changed from what you gave.
  7. It shows the change as before and after, says what it affects, and asks "Save this?" Only your clear yes saves it.

Changes: every change is kept, and can be put back

Flag review: which rules to relax

Open Flag review (managers and admins). For every gate rule: what people did when it fired, and how those jobs went. After a couple of hundred jobs it shows which flags are too strict, without losing the ones that protect us.

  1. 1How many jobs are screened, against the 200 the hints are built for, and the clean passes everything is compared with.
  2. 2Search, and filter by hint, by fail or flag, and by active or retired.
  3. 3How often people continued past the rule, and how many were skipped. Open Reasons and details for the quick reasons picked each way, and for jobs where it was the only rule that fired.
  4. 4How the continued proposals did once sent, against clean passes.
  5. 5The hint: Candidate to relax (usually continued, and those jobs do as well as passes), Keep it (continued, but those jobs rarely get a reply), Doing its job (mostly skipped), Mixed, or Too few jobs yet.

A hint is a starting point. Read the reasons and open the jobs before an admin rewords or retires a rule on the Rules page.

Five habits that keep it working

  1. Paste the whole job page, with the client section. The gate can only judge what it sees.
  2. Update the status the same day: Sent, Viewed, Chat opened, Interview, and the outcome with its reason.
  3. Record Connects, boost and the Loom video on every sent proposal.
  4. Pick a quick reason, and add your own words, when you continue past a flag or a fail. The Flag review counts them, so they decide which rules we relax.
  5. Fix the library, not the proposal. If the writer says something wrong about a project or a profile, correct it in the library so the next proposal is right too.
