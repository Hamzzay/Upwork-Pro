# Writing rules for every type

1. **The first two lines are the whole pitch.** They are all the client sees in the list. Use a detail only this client wrote, and say something useful about it: a risk, a tradeoff or a smarter first step. No stock opener, no project names, no talk about us.
2. **One insight the client did not already have.** Restating their requirements back to them is not an insight.
3. **Proof is specific.** Name what was built, what Stackup owned, one concrete detail, and a verified result if one exists. One strong project beats two weak ones.
4. **Plain sentences.** No comma lists of features. If three or more features appear in a row, cut to the one that matters most for this client.
5. **Commit to their stack.** Name the tools they named. Never offer "X or Y" when they already chose X.
6. **Answer every explicit ask.** If something cannot be answered honestly, flag it before submitting.
7. **Close on one specific, easy question** the client can answer in a line, or a concrete first step for invites and consulting work.
8. **Numbers only from the library.** Never estimate or invent a result.
9. **Length fits the type.** Usually 120 to 200 words for the cover letter.
10. **Sign exactly as the profile record says.**

## House style

* Clear, simple English. Short sentences.
* No dashes of any kind, no emojis, no checkmarks.
* No pricing, estimates or timelines unless the client asks; then use the quote rule module.
* Never mention white label work, such as MD Driven.
* Project links go on their own line after the project's sentences.
